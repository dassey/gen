# COA comparison and approval

## Comparison

The staff evaluates the courses of action against the criteria established
during mission analysis — before the COAs existed. Choosing criteria after the
fact rigs the comparison toward whichever COA the staff already prefers.

Typical criteria: mission accomplishment, risk to force, risk to mission, tempo,
flexibility, simplicity, surprise, sustainment feasibility, command and control
requirements, civil considerations, force protection, intelligence
supportability.

## The decision matrix

Criteria down the side, COAs across the top. Weight the criteria according to
the commander's guidance and record why each weight was assigned. Score each COA
against each criterion — commonly by rank, where 1 is best — multiply by the
weight, and total the columns.

The matrix informs the recommendation. It does not make the decision. A staff
that treats the lowest total as the answer has confused arithmetic with
judgment.

## Advantages and disadvantages

For each COA, state the advantages, the disadvantages, and the risk. Pair every
advantage with what it costs. This is usually the part the commander reads
first.

## The recommendation

State which COA the staff recommends, the criteria that decided it, how the
principal weakness is mitigated, and the risk the commander would be accepting.

## Approval

The commander may approve a COA as briefed, approve it with modifications, or
direct a new one. Approval with modification is the most common outcome and the
modifications must be captured before orders production begins — not discovered
during it.

On approval the commander issues final planning guidance and refines the CCIRs,
and the staff issues the third warning order.
