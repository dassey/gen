# Course of action development

## Screening criteria

Every COA must be:

- **Feasible** — the unit has the time, space, and resources to execute it.
- **Acceptable** — the cost in casualties, equipment, time, and position is
  justified by the expected result.
- **Suitable** — it accomplishes the mission and complies with the higher
  commander's intent.
- **Distinguishable** — it differs meaningfully from the other COAs. Different
  form of maneuver, different decisive point, different use of the reserve,
  different tempo, or different task organization. Three plans that differ only
  in which unit leads are one plan.
- **Complete** — it answers what, when, where, how, and why, and accounts for
  the operation from start through the desired end state.

## The steps

1. Assess relative combat power — by warfighting function, not by counting
   vehicles.
2. Generate options, including at least one that the threat would consider
   unlikely.
3. Array forces, starting with the decisive operation.
4. Develop a broad concept: decisive operation, shaping operations, sustaining
   operations, and the main effort.
5. Assign headquarters and build the task organization.
6. Prepare COA statements and sketches.

## Anatomy of a COA statement

- The form of maneuver or the type of defense.
- The **decisive operation** — the one that directly accomplishes the mission.
- The **shaping operations** — what sets conditions for the decisive operation.
- The **sustaining operations** — what generates and maintains combat power.
- The **main effort** — the unit with priority of support at a given time, and
  when priority shifts.
- The end state.

## Forms of maneuver (offense)

Envelopment, flank attack, frontal attack, infiltration, penetration, turning
movement. Frontal attack is the least preferred and the most costly; it is also
the fastest to arrange, which is why it keeps appearing.

## Types of defense

Area defense (deny terrain), mobile defense (destroy the enemy with a striking
force), and retrograde (delay, withdrawal, retirement). A mobile defense
requires a striking force with greater mobility than the attacker and the
willingness to give ground deliberately.

## Relative combat power

Compare by warfighting function and state a conclusion. A planning ratio of
roughly 3:1 at the point of penetration is the usual starting point for a
deliberate attack against a prepared defense; a defender in prepared positions
can generally hold against roughly 3:1. These are planning aids, not laws, and
they say nothing about morale, training, or surprise.
