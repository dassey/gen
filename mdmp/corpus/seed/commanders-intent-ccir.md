# Commander's intent, CCIRs, and EEFIs

## Commander's intent

A clear and concise expression of the purpose of the operation and the desired
military end state that supports mission command. It has three parts:

- **Expanded purpose** — why the operation matters in the larger fight. Broader
  than the "in order to" of the mission statement.
- **Key tasks** — activities the force must perform as a whole to achieve the
  end state. Key tasks are *conditions the force must achieve*, not tasks
  assigned to named units, and they are not tied to a single course of action.
- **End state** — the conditions that define success, described in terms of
  friendly forces, the threat, the terrain, and civil considerations.

The test of an intent statement: if the plan falls apart and communications are
lost, can a subordinate two levels down read this and act correctly? If not, it
is not finished.

## CCIRs

A commander's critical information requirement is information the commander
identifies as critical to timely decision making. Two kinds:

- **PIR — priority intelligence requirement.** About the threat, terrain,
  weather, or civil considerations. Every PIR must tie to a decision. If nothing
  the commander does would change based on the answer, it is not a PIR.
- **FFIR — friendly force information requirement.** About the friendly force.
  Combat power thresholds, loss of a key capability, commitment of the reserve.

CCIRs are the commander's, not the staff's. They change as the operation
develops, and the list should stay short enough that everyone can remember it.

A good PIR states the question, the decision it supports, and where it will be
answered — a named area of interest and a latest time information is of value.

## EEFIs

Essential elements of friendly information are the things the commander must
keep the threat from learning. **EEFIs are not CCIRs**, but the staff manages
them with the same priority. Typical EEFIs: the location and identity of the
main effort, the timing of the attack, the location and commitment criteria of
the reserve, the locations of command posts.

## Decision points

A decision point is a point in time and space at which the commander must make a
decision. Each one should be linked to a CCIR and to a named area of interest,
and each should carry the latest time the decision can be made and still be
executed.
