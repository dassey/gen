# Intelligence preparation and terrain analysis

## The four steps

1. **Define the operational environment.** Establish the area of operations, the
   area of influence, and the area of interest.
2. **Describe environmental effects on operations.** Terrain, weather, and civil
   considerations — described by their effects on both forces.
3. **Evaluate the threat.** Composition, disposition, strength, doctrine,
   capabilities, and observed patterns.
4. **Determine threat courses of action.** Most likely and most dangerous, each
   with associated named areas of interest, indicators, and a time phase line.

## OAKOC

- **Observation and fields of fire.** What can be seen and engaged from where,
  and out to what range. State it for both sides.
- **Avenues of approach.** Routes into an objective area, classified by the size
  of force they support and by whether they are mounted or dismounted.
- **Key terrain.** Ground whose seizure or control gives a marked advantage to
  either side. Terrain is not key merely because it is high.
- **Obstacles.** Existing and reinforcing. Note whether they block, turn, fix,
  or disrupt.
- **Cover and concealment.** Cover protects from fire; concealment protects from
  observation. They are not the same thing and rarely coincide.

## Weather

Visibility, wind, precipitation, cloud cover and ceiling, temperature and
humidity, and illumination — including BMNT, sunrise, sunset, EENT, moonrise,
moonset, and percentage illumination. As with terrain, the product is the
*effect*: which systems degrade, which windows open and close, and for whom.

## Named areas of interest

A named area of interest is where an indicator will be observed to confirm or
deny a threat course of action. A target area of interest is where the threat
can be engaged to produce a specific effect. Every NAI should tie to a specific
information requirement and a collection asset that can actually cover it.

## Civil considerations — ASCOPE

Areas, structures, capabilities, organizations, people, events. The useful
output is the small set of civil facts that would change a friendly decision,
not an encyclopedia of the district.
