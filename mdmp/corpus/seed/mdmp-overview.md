# The Military Decision-Making Process — overview

MDMP is an iterative planning methodology that integrates the activities of the
commander, the staff, subordinate headquarters, and other partners to understand
a situation and mission, develop and compare courses of action, decide on a
course of action that best accomplishes the mission, and produce an operation
order for execution.

## The seven steps

1. **Receipt of Mission.** Alert the staff, gather the tools, update running
   estimates, conduct an initial assessment, issue the commander's initial
   guidance, and issue the first warning order.
2. **Mission Analysis.** The longest and most important step. Produces the
   problem statement, the restated mission, the initial commander's intent, the
   initial planning guidance, the initial CCIRs and EEFIs, updated IPB products,
   the assumptions, the evaluation criteria, and the second warning order.
3. **COA Development.** Assess relative combat power, generate options, array
   forces, develop a broad concept, assign headquarters, and prepare COA
   statements and sketches.
4. **COA Analysis (war gaming).** Fight each COA against the threat's likely and
   most dangerous actions. Produces refined COAs, decision points, and the
   synchronization matrix.
5. **COA Comparison.** Evaluate the COAs against the criteria established during
   mission analysis and produce a staff recommendation.
6. **COA Approval.** The commander selects a COA, refines the intent, refines
   the CCIRs, and the staff issues the third warning order.
7. **Orders Production, Dissemination, and Transition.** Produce the OPORD,
   disseminate it, conduct confirmation briefs and rehearsals, and transition
   from planning to execution.

## Time management

The 1/3 – 2/3 rule: the headquarters uses no more than one third of the
available time for its own planning, leaving two thirds for subordinates to
conduct their own troop leading procedures. Warning orders exist so subordinates
can begin preparing before the order is finished.

## Abbreviating the process

When time is short the commander directs which steps to abbreviate. Common
abbreviations: develop a single COA rather than several, skip formal COA
comparison, war game only the critical events, or issue a fragmentary order
instead of a complete OPORD. Abbreviating is a command decision and carries
accepted risk — it is not a staff shortcut.

## Common failures

- Treating mission analysis as a briefing to be produced rather than a problem
  to be understood.
- Developing courses of action that differ only cosmetically.
- War gaming to confirm the plan rather than to break it.
- Selecting evaluation criteria after the COAs exist, which rigs the comparison.
- Producing an order the staff understands but subordinates cannot execute.
