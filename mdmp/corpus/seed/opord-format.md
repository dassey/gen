# The five-paragraph operation order

An OPORD is a directive issued by a commander to subordinate commanders to
coordinate the execution of an operation. The five-paragraph format exists so
that anyone in any unit can find the information they need in the place they
expect it.

## Heading

References (maps, charts, datums, and the higher order), the time zone used
throughout the order, and the task organization.

## 1. Situation

- **a. Area of interest.** The area of concern beyond the area of operations,
  including territory from which the threat can influence the operation.
- **b. Area of operations.** Terrain and weather, described by their *effects*
  on friendly and threat operations. Use OAKOC. "There is a ridge" is not
  analysis; "the ridge denies us observation of the eastern approach" is.
- **c. Enemy forces.** Composition, disposition, and strength; recent
  activities; capabilities; most likely course of action; most dangerous course
  of action.
- **d. Friendly forces.** The mission and intent of the headquarters two levels
  up and one level up, then the missions of adjacent units — left, right,
  front, rear, and reserve.
- **e. Interagency, intergovernmental, and nongovernmental organizations.**
- **f. Civil considerations.** ASCOPE.
- **g. Attachments and detachments,** with effective times.
- **h. Assumptions.**

## 2. Mission

The restated mission: who, what, when, where, and why, in one sentence.

## 3. Execution

- **a. Commander's intent.** Expanded purpose, key tasks, end state.
- **b. Concept of operations.** Decisive, shaping, and sustaining operations,
  by phase, naming the main effort and when priority shifts.
- **c. Scheme of movement and maneuver.**
- **d. Scheme of intelligence / information collection.**
- **e. Scheme of fires.**
- **f. Scheme of protection.**
- **g. Tasks to subordinate units.** One subparagraph per subordinate, in task
  organization order, each with a task and a purpose. Every essential task must
  appear against some unit.
- **h. Coordinating instructions.** Instructions applying to two or more units:
  CCIRs, EEFIs, rules of engagement, risk reduction control measures, the
  timeline, rehearsals, and required reports.

## 4. Sustainment

The concept of support. If it cannot carry the concept of operations, the plan
is not feasible.

- **a. Logistics** — maintenance, transportation, supply, field services.
- **b. Personnel** — strength reporting, replacements, casualty operations.
- **c. Health system support** — evacuation, treatment, Role 1 and Role 2
  locations.

## 5. Command and Signal

- **a. Command** — location of the commander and key leaders, succession of
  command, liaison requirements.
- **b. Control** — command post locations and hours, required reports.
- **c. Signal** — the PACE plan (primary, alternate, contingency, emergency),
  net structure, code words, recognition signals, and the trigger for moving
  from one means to the next.

## Closing

Acknowledgement instructions, the commander's signature block, the list of
annexes, and distribution.

## Annexes

A Task Organization, B Intelligence, C Operations, D Fires, E Protection,
F Sustainment, G Engineer, H Signal, J Public Affairs, K Civil Affairs
Operations, L Information Collection, M Assessment, N Space Operations,
P Host-Nation Support, R Reports, U Inspector General, V Interagency
Coordination, W Operational Contract Support, Z Distribution.

Produce only the annexes the operation actually requires.
