# Risk management

Risk management is a continuous process, not a form completed once during
planning.

## The five steps

1. **Identify hazards.** Use the mission variables — METT-TC (I) — as a lens.
2. **Assess hazards to determine risk.** Probability against severity.
3. **Develop controls and make risk decisions.** Controls reduce probability,
   severity, or exposure. The decision to accept residual risk belongs to a
   commander at the appropriate level.
4. **Implement controls.** Controls that exist only in the order are not
   implemented.
5. **Supervise and evaluate.** Conditions change; so does risk.

## Probability

Frequent, Likely, Occasional, Seldom, Unlikely.

## Severity

Catastrophic (death, loss of a system, mission failure), Critical (severe
injury, major damage, significantly degraded mission capability), Moderate
(minor injury, minor damage, degraded capability), Negligible (minimal injury
or damage).

## Risk levels

The intersection of probability and severity gives Extremely High, High,
Moderate, or Low. Extremely high and high residual risk require approval at
higher levels of command.

## Risk to force and risk to mission

They are different and they trade against each other. A COA that minimises risk
to the force by advancing cautiously may raise risk to the mission by arriving
too late. State both, separately, and say which one the commander is accepting.

## Practical notes

- The most common under-assessed hazards in training are casualty evacuation
  timelines, fratricide at boundaries, and sustainment interdiction.
- A control that cannot be resourced is not a control. If the mitigation depends
  on an asset the unit does not have, the risk has not been reduced.
- Residual risk is what remains after controls are implemented. Record it.
