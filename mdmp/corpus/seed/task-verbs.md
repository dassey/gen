# Tactical mission tasks and purposes

The task in a mission statement should be a doctrinal tactical mission task.
Doctrinal verbs mean the same thing to every reader; invented verbs do not.

## Effects on an enemy force

- **Destroy** — physically render a force combat-ineffective until reconstituted.
- **Defeat** — diminish effectiveness to the point the force cannot accomplish
  its mission.
- **Disrupt** — break apart the enemy's formation or tempo, interrupt their
  timetable, or force premature commitment.
- **Neutralize** — render personnel or materiel incapable of interfering with a
  particular operation.
- **Suppress** — temporarily degrade performance below what is needed to
  accomplish the mission.
- **Block** — deny access to an area or prevent advance in a direction.
- **Fix** — prevent the enemy from moving any part of their force from a
  location for a period of time.
- **Isolate** — seal off from sources of support and deny freedom of movement.
- **Canalize** — restrict movement to a narrow zone.
- **Contain** — stop, hold, or surround, or cause the enemy to center activity
  on a given front.
- **Interdict** — prevent, disrupt, or delay the enemy's use of an area or route.

## Actions by a friendly force

Attack, seize, secure, clear, occupy, retain, breach, bypass, infiltrate,
exfiltrate, follow and assume, follow and support, screen, guard, cover,
support by fire, attack by fire, conduct a movement to contact, defend, delay,
conduct a relief in place, conduct a passage of lines.

Note the difference between **seize** (take by force) and **secure** (prevent
damage or destruction) — securing a bridge after seizing it is a second task,
and plans routinely forget it.

Note the difference between **screen** (early warning), **guard** (fight to gain
time while observing and reporting), and **cover** (operate independently to
intercept, engage, delay, disorganize, and deceive). They demand very different
combat power.

## Purpose verbs

The "in order to" half of a mission statement: allow, cause, create, deceive,
deny, divert, enable, envelop, influence, open, prevent, protect, support,
surprise.

## Task and purpose

Every task assigned to a subordinate carries a purpose. The purpose is what
allows a subordinate to improvise correctly when the task becomes impossible.
A task without a purpose produces a unit that does the wrong thing precisely.
