# The threat and the opposing force

Training scenarios use a doctrinal opposing force so that the threat behaves
consistently and can be templated, taught, and fought.

## Threat postures

- **Peer.** Comparable capability across most warfighting functions. Contests
  every domain. Integrated air defense, long-range fires, electromagnetic
  attack, and its own reconnaissance-strike complex.
- **Near-peer.** Overmatch in selected capabilities — usually fires, unmanned
  systems, or electronic warfare — with gaps in sustainment or mobility.
- **Hybrid.** Regular forces operating alongside irregular forces, criminal
  networks, and information operations. Deniability is a weapon.
- **Irregular.** Guerrilla, insurgent, and criminal elements. Avoids decisive
  engagement; attacks sustainment, isolated elements, and the population's
  confidence in the force.

## Templating a threat course of action

The **most likely course of action** is what the threat's own doctrine tells
them to do, adjusted for the terrain and their observed behaviour — not what
would be most convenient to plan against.

The **most dangerous course of action** is the most damaging option that remains
possible. It is not the least likely thing imaginable. A useful test: the MDCOA
should attack the friendly plan at its most vulnerable moment — the transition,
the consolidation, the passage of lines, the resupply.

Both must be consistent with the task organization and capabilities assigned to
the threat. A threat that cannot mass a battalion cannot counterattack with one.

## Capabilities that shape planning

Long-range and counter-battery fires; persistent unmanned aerial reconnaissance
cued to fires; electromagnetic attack against communications and navigation;
short-range air defense denying rotary-wing support; scatterable mines and rapid
obstacle emplacement; information operations that publicise civilian harm within
hours.

## Under-templated threats

Two threat approaches routinely surprise units in training:

1. **Attacking sustainment rather than maneuver.** Unmanned systems, long-range
   fires, and irregular elements against the support area, distribution routes,
   and casualty evacuation. Maneuver units become combat-ineffective within days
   without ever losing a fight.
2. **Accepting a penetration deliberately** in order to strike the penetrating
   force when it is extended and least supported.
