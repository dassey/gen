# Warning orders, briefings, and rehearsals

## Warning orders

A warning order gives subordinates advance notice so they can begin their own
planning and preparation in parallel. Issuing them late is the single most
common way a headquarters steals time from its subordinates.

**WARNORD #1** (after receipt of mission): type of operation, general location,
initial timeline, and any movement or reconnaissance to initiate now.

**WARNORD #2** (after mission analysis): restated mission, commander's intent,
area of operations and area of interest, CCIRs and EEFIs, risk guidance,
priorities by warfighting function, military deception guidance, essential
stability tasks, initial information collection plan, updated timeline, and
rehearsal guidance.

**WARNORD #3** (after COA approval): mission, commander's intent, updated CCIRs
and EEFIs, task organization, a sketch of the concept of operations, tasks to
subordinate units, coordinating instructions, and the updated timeline and
rehearsal plan.

## Briefings

- **Mission analysis briefing** — presents the staff's understanding of the
  problem so the commander can correct it before COAs are built.
- **COA briefing** — presents developed COAs for the commander to refine or
  reject before the war game.
- **COA decision briefing** — presents the comparison and the recommendation.
- **Confirmation brief** — subordinate commanders back-brief their understanding
  of the task, purpose, and their part in the concept. Given immediately after
  receiving the order.

## Rehearsals

Types, in decreasing order of resource cost: full dress, reduced force, terrain
model, sketch map, map, and radio. The type is chosen by the time available, not
by preference.

Common rehearsals:

- **Combined arms rehearsal** — synchronises the warfighting functions across
  the force against the threat's most likely course of action.
- **Support rehearsal** — sustainment and medical, usually run by the support
  operations officer.
- **Fires rehearsal** — targets, triggers, clearance of fires, counter-fire.
- **Battle drill rehearsal** — company and below, executed continuously.
- **Communications rehearsal** — every station demonstrates the PACE jump. A
  PACE plan that has been briefed but not rehearsed will fail on first contact.

Rehearse against the threat, not against a script. A rehearsal in which nothing
goes wrong has told you nothing.
